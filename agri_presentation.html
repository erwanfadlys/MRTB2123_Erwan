Theoretical Linkage

The Information Wheel & Decision Models

Operational data moves from raw signal to actionable knowledge, shaping how farms forecast, allocate, and decide.

Demand Side

CSFs such as optimal harvesting windows determine the sensor questions.

Critical success factors like maturity timing, spoilage risk, and field readiness drive what needs to be sensed, how often it must be observed, and which anomalies matter most.

Supply Side

Raw data is the new oil only after it is refined into knowledge.

Soil, weather, and machinery streams gain strategic value only once cleaned, contextualised, and transformed into reliable information for agronomic and operational action.

Decision Modelling

Predictive structures forecast crop volume and decision consequences.

Decision models quantify expected yield response, planning tradeoffs, and intervention timing so managers act earlier with clearer confidence.

Predictive Analytics

Weather and soil patterns become a forward planning horizon.

Predictive analytics extrapolates rainfall, moisture stress, nutrient dynamics, and field variability to estimate crop performance before the season closes.

Planning horizon · field foresight

Stochastic Prescriptive Models

Optimal irrigation is chosen under climate uncertainty, not despite it.

Stochastic prescriptive models determine irrigation and resource policies that maximise expected profit while respecting volatile rainfall, temperature shocks, and supply constraints.

Profit maximisation · uncertainty-aware control

Regression Modelling: Yield Prediction with Interaction Terms

Model Specification
Y = Crop Yield (t/ha)
W = Water (mm)
N = Nitrogen (kg/ha)
★ Interaction Term β₃(W×N)
Captures synergy: water and nitrogen jointly amplify yield beyond their individual effects.
Variable Coeff. Std.Err p-value
Intercept (β₀) 1.24 0.31 0.001 ***
Water β₁ 0.58 0.09 <0.001 ***

W×N β₃ ★ 0.29 0.07 0.002 ***
Temperature β₄ −0.17 0.05 0.021 **
R² = 0.91 - Model explains 91% of yield variance | F(4,145) = 78.3, p < 0.001

The positive interaction coefficient (β₃ = 0.29) confirms that optimal nitrogen amplifies the yield response to water - justifying joint IoT monitoring of both inputs.
